1.15. The horses in Lean🔗

This is the case from the opening, built in Lean. It does a second job at the same time. Suppose Rothbard's own six horses satisfy swap dominance (SwapDominant) and every condition of the law's chain form (marginal_utility_chain) at once. Then those assumptions can all hold together. So the law, in its chain form, is not empty.

Wants are ranks on the man's value scale. The smaller rank number is the more urgent want:

🔗def
Apodictic.Model.rankPrefers (X Y : Set Model.Want) : Prop
Apodictic.Model.rankPrefers
  (X Y : Set Model.Want) : Prop

Preference between bundles of wants. X is preferred to Y exactly when two things hold. The two bundles differ by a single swap. And the want X has in place of Y's is the more urgent of the two. Here more urgent just means a smaller rank number.

The man's plan is the obvious one. With any n horses he would serve the n most urgent wants. This plan satisfies swap dominance (SwapDominant). Five more things hold here:

  • Wants can be told apart (DecidableEq praxis.End).

  • Uses are independent (IndependentUses agent time).

  • One-horse steps exist inside the stable (stock.OneMore).

  • Each step has a marginal end.

  • Preference is asymmetric (AsymmetricPreference).

The last one matters. With it, swap dominance (SwapDominant) holds somewhere preference is strict. That is how swap dominance was meant all along. no_rival_swap_dominant needs asymmetry too.

Two of the theorems here are not bookkeeping. They are conclusions Rothbard reached himself. The first is his result at p. 25:

🔗theorem
Apodictic.Model.loss_of_a_horse_ends_pleasure_riding :
  Model.pleasureRiding ∈
    marginalEnds (Model.horsePlan 6) (Finset.range 5) (Finset.range 6)
Apodictic.Model.loss_of_a_horse_ends_pleasure_riding :
  Model.pleasureRiding ∈
    marginalEnds (Model.horsePlan 6)
      (Finset.range 5) (Finset.range 6)

Rothbard's own conclusion, p. 25. With six horses the man would serve the six most urgent wants. With five he would not serve the sixth-ranked want. So the sixth-ranked want is one he gives up when a horse goes. In Rothbard's telling it is pleasure riding: "the loss of a horse will cause him to give up pleasure riding".

Here it is a calculation about the plan, not a further assumption.

The second is the point he names two horses to make. It is also the reason the plan is encoded the way it is:

🔗theorem
Apodictic.Model.which_horse_does_not_matter :
  (Model.horsePlan 6).wouldServe
      ((Finset.range 6).erase Model.manOWar) =
    (Model.horsePlan 6).wouldServe
      ((Finset.range 6).erase Model.seabiscuit)
Apodictic.Model.which_horse_does_not_matter :
  (Model.horsePlan 6).wouldServe
      ((Finset.range 6).erase
        Model.manOWar) =
    (Model.horsePlan 6).wouldServe
      ((Finset.range 6).erase
        Model.seabiscuit)

Rothbard's own conclusion, p. 27. It is the one he names two horses to make. Man o' War was leading a wagon. Seabiscuit was out for pleasure riding. After losing Man o' War, the man would serve exactly the ends he would serve after losing Seabiscuit. In Rothbard's telling, he simply moves Seabiscuit to the wagon. Which of the two goes makes no difference to which end is given up.

This is what interchangeability amounts to. It is why the plan is indexed by which horses, while Homogeneous is a separate named condition. Rothbard's point has to be statable before it can be true.

And the last step is the point:

🔗theorem
Apodictic.Model.horses_law_applies (k n : ℕ) (h : n < k)
  (h' : n + 1 < k) (addedFirst : Model.Horses.End) :
  addedFirst ∈
      marginalEnds (Model.horsePlan k) (Finset.range n)
        (Finset.range (n + 1)) →
    ∀
      addedSecond ∈
        marginalEnds (Model.horsePlan k) (Finset.range (n + 1))
          (Finset.range (n + 2)),
      Model.Horses.PrefersEnd () () addedFirst addedSecond
Apodictic.Model.horses_law_applies
  (k n : ℕ) (h : n < k) (h' : n + 1 < k)

        (Finset.range n)

        marginalEnds (Model.horsePlan k)
          (Finset.range (n + 1))

      Model.Horses.PrefersEnd () ()

The law itself, applied to the horses. Every hypothesis of marginal_utility_chain is met at this frame. So the claim and the conditions on the situation can all hold at once, and the law is not empty. That is the whole consistency argument. This type-checks, so they fit together.

That is the law itself, applied to the horses. Every assumption of the chain form is met. The size-based form (marginal_utility) asks for one thing more: interchangeability (plan.Homogeneous). The horses meet that too:

🔗theorem
Apodictic.Model.horses_homogeneous (k : ℕ) :
  (Model.horsePlan k).Homogeneous
Apodictic.Model.horses_homogeneous
  (k : ℕ) :
  (Model.horsePlan k).Homogeneous

Interchangeability holds of the plan. The plan depends on how many horses there are. It never depends on which.

No theorem here applies the size-based form to them.

Nothing was copied across by hand. horses_swapDominant proves SwapDominant (horsePlan k), exactly the hypothesis marginal_utility_chain takes (dominance).

🔗theorem
Apodictic.Model.horses_swapDominant (k : ℕ) :
  SwapDominant (Model.horsePlan k)
Apodictic.Model.horses_swapDominant
  (k : ℕ) :
  SwapDominant (Model.horsePlan k)

The man's plan is swap-dominant. This is the claim itself, proved of this frame and this plan. Serving the n most urgent wants beats any one-swap alternative. That is because a want a horse can serve, but the plan would leave out, is less urgent than every want the plan would serve. It has a larger rank number.

The model departs from Rothbard in two places. Both are said out loud rather than papered over.

  • No ceiling on the scale. His ten ends are "for simplicity", to fit a diagram. A ceiling would drag an extra condition through every theorem, and it would gain nothing. So here the horses can serve any rank.

  • Which earlier horse is Man o' War is arbitrary. Rothbard says only that he arrived before Seabiscuit. So any earlier horse will do. The plan depends only on how many horses there are (horses_homogeneous), and nothing about a horse but its identity enters any claim.
