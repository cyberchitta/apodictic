1.5. The conditions🔗

The law of marginal utility takes five conditions in all, across its routes here, besides the claims. Each says something about the situation, not about action as such. Each is written into the statement. So a reader can point at one and say: that is the one that did not hold. Where one fails, the law says nothing. It is silent, not wrong.

  • One more unit (stock.OneMore fewer more). The two piles compared are both on hand. They differ by exactly one unit. The plan says nothing about piles outside the man's stock. Neither does the law.

  • Independence of uses (IndependentUses agent time). What one want is worth does not depend on which other wants are being served.

  • Interchangeability of units (plan.Homogeneous). The plan depends only on how many units there are, not on which ones. Of the law's forms, only the size-based one needs it.

  • Comparability (stock.ComparableServiceable). Any two wants the good can serve are ranked one way or the other. Mises's route needs it. So does the ladder read as a history. Rothbard's route never does.

  • No new uses (earlier.NoNewUses later). At the later moment, the good is believed to serve no use it was not believed to serve at the earlier one. Only the ladder read as a history needs it. There it is the one condition that spans two moments. It is about belief in what the good can do, not about the scale. And it runs one way. The man learns no new use, though he may drop an old one.

Nothing here has to say which plan is the man's. A theorem is handed a plan. Its statement is about that plan. So there is no rival plan anyone could build to refute the theorem.

All five are given in full here, in the order they are listed above. They are given in full because these are the conditions a reader has to judge.

Other parts take conditions of their own:

  • Exchange takes conditions about trades rather than stocks. They are given in full under Exchange.

  • Time preference takes its own too, about dated ends. They are given in full under Time preference.

  • The regression theorem's are about a record of exchanges. They are given in full under The regression theorem.

  • The interest theorems' are about sums of money and a loan. They are given in full under Interest. Some of the interest theorems take interchangeability of units (plan.Homogeneous) too, for promises of future money.

Independence of uses — a condition on the situation, NOT a universal claim. Take two bundles that differ in exactly one slot. Then preferring one bundle to the other carries down to preferring the one end to the other. It fails where uses are complementary: where B is worth having only alongside A. It is named here so that the theorems that need it carry it as a hypothesis. It is also named so that a reader can point at it where it does not hold.

This is NOT a premise Rothbard spends. He ranks wants against each other directly. He ranks them off a scale that is already ranked (MES pp. 25–26, Figure 3). He never argues from bundles at all. This condition is what OUR decomposition costs. Where he makes one fused claim, we make a claim about bundles (SwapDominant) plus this condition. His wording never has to face complementarity. Once the pieces are pulled apart, complementarity lands here. To assert this condition rather than hypothesize it would be to assert that complementarity never happens.

Interchangeability of units — a condition on the situation, NOT a praxeological claim. The plan depends only on how many units there are, not on which ones. Any two sub-stocks of the same size would serve the same ends.

Without it, the phrase "the plan at n units" picks out nothing in particular. So neither does the marginal utility of a supply of n. Rothbard makes interchangeability part of what a supply IS: "If a specific unit is differently evaluated from all other units, then the supply of that good is only one unit" (MES p. 23). Where it fails, the units are not one good. Then the supply-size form of the law does not treat them as one. Like the plan it constrains, this condition speaks of sub-stocks the agent may not hold.

Comparability of the ends a good can serve — a condition on the situation, NOT a praxeological claim.

Any two ends the good can serve are ranked one way or the other. It is confined to one stock's serviceable ends, because that is all any theorem needs. It is not asserted of every pair of ends in the frame.

Where it fails, a theorem carrying it is SILENT rather than false. An agent may simply never have had occasion to rank two ends against each other. Rothbard's own doctrine is that scales are revealed in action and nowhere else. That doctrine makes such agents the normal case rather than the exotic one. Mises says the same thing in almost the same words: "the scale of values or wants manifests itself only in the reality of action. These scales have no independent existence apart from the actual behavior of individuals" (Human Action, ch. IV, §2).

It is carried by the Mises route (Apodictic.Mises.ladder_from_order). It is carried by the ladder read as a history (Apodictic.Temporal.ladder_judged_earlier). It is carried by no theorem on the Rothbard route. That asymmetry is a finding. Swap dominance (SwapDominant) forces a comparison between every served end and every unserved serviceable one. So this condition can only fail for a pair that never straddles a margin. See Apodictic.Model.rothbard_applies_where_mises_is_silent.

The good is believed to serve no new use — whatever the good is believed to serve at the later moment, it was believed to serve at the earlier one. The later stock's serviceable ends are among the earlier stock's.

It is a situational condition. It is the one condition on the history reading that spans two moments. It runs one way. It is about BELIEF about the good, not about the scale of values. Between the two moments the man learns no new use for the good. The condition is there so that the want the added unit goes to was on the earlier scale at all. Where he does learn one, the earlier-judged ladder is silent.

Does not say that the good's uses stay the same. A use believed in earlier may be dropped later. It says nothing about the scale at either moment. It says nothing about which units are held, or about the order of the two times. It is carried by Apodictic.Temporal.ladder_judged_earlier and by nothing else.
