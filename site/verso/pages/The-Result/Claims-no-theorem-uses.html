1.17. Claims no theorem uses🔗

One claim at the centre of the doctrine is carried by no theorem here. It is the claim that there is any action at all (Mises.ActionOccurs). So it is not among the ten claims under The claims.

Demonstrated preference in Rothbard's sense (DemonstratedPreference) is not unused. It is the bridge from what a man actually does to what he prefers. It is stated of one trade. It is the fifth claim under The claims. Theorems in three parts carry it:

  • Exchange (MutualBenefit.reverse_valuations and the other exchange theorems).

  • The regression theorem (Regression.indirect_ranks_onward_ends).

  • Interest (PureInterest.struck_rate_positive).

Action itself is a definition here (Action). No derivation of the law uses it. The existence claim has been tried once. Mises appeals to it to close his dilemma. A frame with exactly one action shows that the appeal falls short of what he needs. The details are under Mises's route.

🔗structure
Apodictic.Action (praxis : ActionFrame) : Type
Apodictic.Action (praxis : ActionFrame) :
  Type

An action. An agent, at a time, uses means. He believes the means will bring about a chosen end. By acting he gives up at least one alternative end.

The fields hold together as a package. Nothing with fewer parts counts as an action. But this is a definition, so it asserts nothing. Shape: our-reconstruction.

No derivation of the law uses this structure. On both routes the law rests on the counterfactual plan alone. The structure does appear in Mises's dilemma (Apodictic.Mises.ActionOccurs, Apodictic.Mises.ActionRecurs). The dilemma is machine-checked not to deliver the law.
