1.3. The other findings🔗

The audit turned up more than the one claim. The findings fall into three groups.

What the derivation is, and what it delivers.

  • Rothbard's derivation is one step from an asserted premise (p. 24). The one-step proof is faithful to him. The audit point is elsewhere. He calls the law "derived from the fundamental axiom of human action" (p. 27). That derivation rests on an assertion.

  • The law is strict, as Rothbard's is. Non-increasing marginal utility is the neoclassical form. It is not his.

  • Nothing assumes that exactly one want is given up. Rothbard's phrase "the marginal unit" takes that for granted. The law here holds for every want that goes.

  • One unit to one end is not needed at all. Rothbard assumes it. He flags the assumption: "each unit of means is capable of serving one of the ends". He prefaces it "We assume for simplicity" (pp. 25–26). It reads like a premise of the derivation. It is not a premise of anything here. It was built into what a plan is, where no statement showed it. It has since been taken out of the plan (AllocationPlan). No theorem asked for it back. Take a stock whose next unit opens no further want. The law passes over that case in silence. The six horses are not such a case: there the law speaks. So nothing is lost. It is a simplification its own author flagged. The ordering does not want it.

  • The proof never uses the fact that the end at the smaller supply is the marginal one. The law holds for every end served there. It holds against every end the next unit would add. Something stronger holds still. A served end beats any unserved end that could be served (served_over_unserved), not only the one the next unit reaches. Rothbard's statement claims less than his premise delivers.

What the law does not need. This is most of what it usually gets.

  • No property of preference is forced. Transitivity is not forced. Neither is completeness. Rothbard assumes a single ranked value scale (Figure 3, pp. 25–26). None of it is needed, because the plan already carries the ordering.

  • Interchangeability of units (plan.Homogeneous) is not needed for the ordering. Here the plan is indexed by which units. With that, two results go through without interchangeability: the urgency principle (urgency_principle) and the law along a chain of named units (marginal_utility_chain). Among the law's theorems, it is needed exactly once: to state the law by size of supply (plan.Homogeneous). The phrase "the plan at n units" picks out one thing only if two piles of the same size serve the same ends. So interchangeability bears on the wording, not on the derivation. That is why it is a hypothesis and not a praxeological claim. Rothbard makes it part of what a supply is (p. 23). On his definition, a stock where it fails is not one good but several. But his definition does not deliver it. It speaks of units "equally capable of rendering the same service". That is a fact about the units. The wording needs a fact about the man's plan over them. One stock carries plans of both kinds (unitsAlike_not_entail_homogeneous). So no condition on the units can decide it.

  • Indifference between units is needed nowhere. Rothbard defines a supply with the words "valued equally" and "regards ... indifferently" (p. 23). He takes them back on the next page: interchangeability "does not mean that the concrete units are actually valued equally" (p. 24). The formalization follows p. 24.

  • Rothbard does say which unit goes, three times. Each time he gets the answer from interchangeability. In none of the three does he state a condition on the plan (plan.Homogeneous). With six horses: "As a result of the interchangeability of units, it does not matter to him which of the six units he must lose" (pp. 26–27). In the recapitulation of fundamentals: "The definition of a good is that it consists of an interchangeable supply of one or more units. Therefore, every unit will always be valued equally with every other" (p. 320). And a lost unit costs the lowest-ranked use "regardless of which end the unit is supplying at present" (p. 458).

  • That "Therefore" is the step the counter-model refutes (unitsAlike_not_entail_homogeneous). Units can be alike in what they can do. That does not make a plan blind to which of them it has. The charge does not need him to have shifted the word's meaning, either. Grant the usual reconciliation. On it, p. 24 is about units taken on one after another. Page 320 is about units in a stock, each valued at the margin. Both readings are still facts about the units or their valuation. The size-based wording needs a fact about the plan over sub-stocks. Prose has no way to mark that distinction. The machine would not leave it unmarked.

  • Independence of uses is a condition on the situation (IndependentUses agent time). It can be stated at all only once preference ranges over bundles of ends rather than single ends. Where uses are complementary, the law says nothing. The statement admits as much.

About the formalizing, not the doctrine.

  • The phrase "this bundle, minus this end" hides a premise: that two ends can be told apart in the first place (DecidableEq praxis.End). It came to light only because the proofs refuse classical logic.

  • The claim that people act splits into three. There is a definition (Action). There is a bridge (DemonstratedPreference). And there is an existence claim (Mises.ActionOccurs). Only the bridge could do deductive work. The law of marginal utility never needs it. Exchange does. There it enters stated of one trade (The claims).

  • The ladder as a history uses a claim about what a man actually does (ActsInOrder). That claim is not the bridge. It runs from his ranking to his act. It says nothing about what an act shows he prefers.

  • Never assert a claim about every structure of a given shape: say, every plan the man might have. Hand someone one plan. They can cook up a neighbour of it that breaks the claim. So a claim of that form is refutable by construction. Make it about the one plan the theorem actually receives. Then the same construction is harmless. Better: it turns into a result. One of the theorems below takes asymmetric preference (AsymmetricPreference). Given that, it proves that two plans satisfying swap dominance (SwapDominant) cannot differ by a single swap (no_rival_swap_dominant). So "the" value scale is something proved here, not something assumed.
