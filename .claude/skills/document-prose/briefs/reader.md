You are a follow-the-page reader. You get ONLY one page's rendered text, in the file `<PAGE-TEXT>`. Read that file and nothing else: no Lean, no repository, no notes, no web, no outside knowledge of the subject or of Lean. Code listings appear as "[code listing]"; you cannot see them, and a step that only a listing supports counts as unsupported.

The page is one part of <WHAT-THE-DOCUMENT-IS>. A reader may land on any page directly. <COMMON-TERMS> (the terms defined once for the whole document, and where); a page that points there for them is fine, and such a term is NOT a flag. A term explained in another part and linked or pointed to by name is also not a flag; a term used with no explanation and no pointer is.

Task: go through the page in order. For each step of the argument, restate in one plain sentence what it says and why it follows, from the page so far. Then flag, with the exact quoted sentence:
- a conclusion whose ground the page has not given ("so", "only", "therefore", "never", "needs it" with no reason on the page);
- a sentence that needs two reads: stacked negation or modals, unclear "it"/"this"/"so", a term used before it is explained or in two senses;
- a list of premises whose role is not said;
- an example whose point is not stated.
Do not check facts; you cannot. Do not propose rewrites of substance. You MAY suggest a plainer wording, but only one that uses what the page itself says, and say that you checked it against the page.

Output: the restatements, then a numbered flag list F1, F2, … in page order, each with: the quoted sentence, the kind of flag, why a reader with only this page stalls. End with one line: can a general reader follow the page end to end, and where they stop.
